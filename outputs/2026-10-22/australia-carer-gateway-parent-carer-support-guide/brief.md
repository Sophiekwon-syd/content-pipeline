Explain carer as a person who provides unpaid care because another person has disability, mental illness, a medical condition or age-related frailty. This article focuses on a parent caring for a child with disability or a long-term condition. The eligibility checker asks about the caring situation, living in Australia and support needs. Individual circumstances should be discussed with the service. Sources: [Carer Gateway eligibility checker](https://www.carergateway.gov.au/about-us/eligibility-checker), [caring for a child](https://www.carergateway.gov.au/your-life-carer/your-life-around-caring/who-caring/caring-child).

Distinguish this service from ordinary age-related childcare. Do not imply every parent qualifies because parenting is demanding. ABS distinguishes ordinary care of young children from assistance needed because of disability: [ABS SDAC methodology](https://www.abs.gov.au/methodologies/disability-ageing-and-carers-australia-summary-findings-methodology/2022).

Define respite once as another person temporarily taking over care so the usual carer can have a break. Planned respite may be available through a tailored support package; do not imply automatic availability or promise hours, cash amounts or regular cleaning. Source: [Carer Gateway respite guidance](https://www.carergateway.gov.au/help-and-support/caring-me/available-support-carers/planned-emergency-respite).

In-person and telephone counselling options exist. If describing direct phone-counselling booking, the official page specifies option 2 and a service for carers over 18. The practical entry point in this brief remains local-provider option 1. Do not conflate the telephone menu choices. Source: [Carer Gateway counselling](https://www.carergateway.gov.au/help-and-support/caring-me/talk-and-connect/counselling-carers).
